# Aon plc (AON) — Deep Dive at $269.45 (October 2026)

**Question:** Is it safe to buy AON at $269.45 for a 10-year hold?
**Short answer:** At this price the **business risk is low**, the **balance-sheet risk is moderate and temporary**, and the **valuation risk is low**. The main danger is short-term: the stock is in a downtrend at its 52-week low with news still to come (Q3 earnings, the USI deal closing, credit-rating decisions). It's a sound 10-year buy if you **buy in tranches** and accept that it could fall another 10–20% first.

## 2. What the business looks like right now (Q2 2026)
- **Organic revenue growth was 5%**, and all four lines (Commercial Risk, Reinsurance, Health, Wealth) grew about 5%. That happened while commercial insurance prices *fell* ~6% industry-wide.
- **Adjusted operating margin was 28.9%** (+70 basis points). **Adjusted EPS +9%. Free cash flow was $483M** in a seasonally light quarter.
- **2026 guidance reaffirmed:** organic growth of mid-single digits or better, 70–80 basis points of margin expansion, and double-digit free-cash-flow growth.

**Read-through:** the core business is healthy. It is growing through a soft insurance market, with no underwriting risk and high client retention. The stock's fall reflects the *deal*, the *sector* and *sentiment*, not a business that is deteriorating.

## 4. Moat check: is it still intact?
- **Efficient scale and oligopoly.** Large-company risk placement is concentrated among Marsh, Aon, WTW and Gallagher. It needs global reach, data and leverage with insurers. **Intact.**
- **Switching costs.** Multi-year relationships with corporate risk managers and high retention. **Intact.**
- **Asset-light, no underwriting risk.** Aon is paid commissions and fees and doesn't pay claims. **Intact.**
- **Data and analytics** (reinsurance modeling, health and benefits benchmarks). AI is more likely to help incumbents with proprietary data than to replace them in complex placements. **Intact, but watch the small-commercial and middle-market business that USI adds.**

**Verdict:** the moat is wide and intact. The USI deal adds a slightly *narrower*-moat business (middle market) on top of a wide-moat core.

## 5. Valuation: what is it worth?
**Historical context:** Aon's adjusted P/E was mostly ~20–28x over 2019–2024. At ~14–15x it is at its cheapest in over a decade, in line with the whole broker group: Marsh at ~16.5x forward, also "well below its 10-year average".

**Tail risk** (deep recession plus a downgrade to BBB+ plus a valuation de-rating): ~11x on ~$19 = **~$210 (−22%)**. That is painful, but brokers' earnings were resilient in 2008–09, so a permanent loss of that size over 10 years needs a broken moat, not just a bad cycle.

**Analyst targets after the deal:** Mizuho $398 (Outperform), Cantor $433, TD Cowen reiterated Buy. The average target is ~40%+ above the current price.

**Margin of safety:** fair value ~$360–395 against a price of $269 gives a **~25–32% discount**. That meets the Buffett and Pabrai "margin of safety" test.

## 6. Signals from insiders and smart money
- **Chairman Lester Knight bought 20,000 shares ($6.55M) at $327.48 on Sep 2, 2026, after the deal was announced.** Insiders buy for one reason. You would be paying ~18% less than he did.
- Owned by ~10 superinvestors (Dataroma). Berkshire exited in Q1 2026, as part of Greg Abel's clean-out of small positions, before the deal and the price drop.
- CEO Greg Case's reasoning for using debt: "the stock is too cheap to issue equity." Management is effectively saying it considers the shares undervalued.

## 7. Key risks, ranked
1. **Integration and leverage (most important).** It's a $17B deal right after the $13.4B NFP acquisition in 2024. If leverage isn't back near 3x by 2028, expect a downgrade and a lower valuation. *Watch:* debt paydown each quarter, USI organic growth, synergy progress.
2. **Capital-allocation track record.** Aon has made big, bold deals: the WTW merger was blocked in 2021 with a $1B break fee, then NFP, now USI. Paying ~22x EBITDA for USI while Aon itself trades at ~13–14x EBITDA is a questionable use of capital.
3. **A long soft market.** If commercial pricing keeps falling, organic growth could slide to ~3%.
4. **AI in small and mid-size accounts.** More relevant after USI than before.
5. **Short-term momentum.** The stock is making new lows ahead of earnings, and nothing stops it falling to $240–250 first.

## 8. Is $269.45 a good entry?
- **For a 10-year hold: yes.** Price is ~25–30% below my fair value, the moat is intact, the core business is still growing ~5%, and an insider is buying.
- **For an all-in purchase on Monday: no.** The balance-sheet overhang and the October earnings report could push it lower. Buy in stages.

### Suggested Monday execution ($1,500 monthly budget)
- **Tranche 1 (Monday):** ~$300–550 (1–2 shares). Use a **limit order ≤ $275**. If it opens above **$290** (the top of my buy zone), wait.
- **Tranche 2:** after Q3 earnings in late October, *if* organic growth is ≥4% and the USI timeline and debt plan are on track.
- **Tranche 3:** at **≤ $255** (strong-buy zone), or after the USI deal closes and the first debt-paydown quarter is reported.
- **Cap:** keep AON at ≤ 20% of your portfolio. Even a strong conviction can be wrong on leverage.

### Thesis-break triggers (sell or stop adding)
- Organic growth below 3% for two or more quarters.
- Leverage not falling toward ~3.5x within 18 months of close, or a downgrade below BBB+.
- Synergy targets cut, or large USI producer departures (key salespeople leaving).
- Another large debt-funded acquisition before leverage normalizes.

### Practical notes
- **Aon plc is incorporated in Ireland** (listed on the NYSE). Dividend withholding depends on your country and paperwork. It matters little at a ~1% yield.
- Shares of a non-US company may not count as US-situated assets for US estate tax purposes. Confirm with a tax adviser if that is relevant to you.
