# Uber (UBER) — Is It Safe to Wheel? (October 2026)

**Short answer:** Uber suits the wheel *mechanically*: options trade heavily with tight spreads, premiums are rich, and collateral is ~$6,250–6,500 per contract. On *safety*, though, it is a **speculative wheel, not a sleep-well wheel**, because its 10-year moat now depends on how robotaxis play out. It's acceptable as a **small slot (≤15–20% of the account)** with conservative strikes. It shouldn't be your core wheel, and **you can't start it with $1,500**: one put needs ~$6,250.

> Educational, not personalized advice. Option prices are **Black-Scholes estimates** (volatility assumed at 40–45% because Uber is near its lows and earnings are coming; recent realized volatility was ~46%). Check your broker's option chain for real quotes.

## 2. The moat question: robotaxis
**The bear case (real):**
- **Waymo** runs in ~10 metro areas, is targeting 1M rides a week, and plans 7 more US cities plus London in 2026. In San Francisco it took **about a quarter of ride-hailing in ~20 months**, mostly through **its own app**.
- **Tesla** is scaling Cybercab, though even Musk calls the rollout "agonizingly slow."
- If autonomous-vehicle owners run their own networks, Uber loses the most profitable part of the value chain. Its moat (driver supply plus rider demand) matters less when cars don't need drivers.

**The bull case (also real):**
- Uber is positioning as the **demand aggregator** for many robotaxi makers. It has committed **$10B+**: ~$2.5B in equity stakes plus ~$7.5B in vehicle purchase commitments.
  - **Lucid and Nuro:** 35,000+ Lucid Gravity robotaxis, with a Bay Area launch planned for 2026.
  - **Nvidia:** targeting 100,000 autonomous vehicles from 2027.
  - **Rivian:** up to 50,000 autonomous R2s from 2028.
- Fleet owners need high use of each car, and Uber has the riders. Uber One membership and **Uber Eats delivery** (less exposed to robotaxis) add stickiness.
- The CEO put $10M of his own money in at ~$71.

**My view:** the moat is narrow and under threat. The outcome in 5–10 years is uncertain. Uber may win as the aggregator, or end up a lower-margin marketplace, and nobody can know yet. That fails the "hold 10 years without worrying" test.

## 6. If you do it: rules for Uber
1. **Collateral:** $6,250 for a $62.50 put. You'll need ~3 more months of savings. Doing this means **redirecting money away from SPGI/AON buying**, which is a real trade-off.
2. **Strike ≤ $62.50.** That's below the CEO's ~$71 purchase and the $65.41 52-week low. Only sell a put if you'd be happy owning 100 shares at ~$60 for years.
3. **Delta 0.15–0.20.** No higher; this stock can gap.
4. **Avoid earnings week (~Nov 2–3)** for your first cycles. Use expiries that end before it, or wait until after.
5. **Size:** Uber ≤ 15–20% of the account. It's a satellite position, not the core.
6. **If assigned:** sell calls at or above $67.50 (above cost basis) with delta ≤ 0.25. If the robotaxi story worsens and the stock drops below $55, **stop selling calls.** Either hold the shares as a value position or exit if the thesis breaks.
7. **Thesis-break signals:** gross bookings growth below 10%, trips flat, or Waymo/Tesla taking material share in more cities *without* using Uber.

## 7. Recommendation
- **Monday:** keep the plan. AON with a limit order ≤ $275, or SPGI ≤ $390 if AON opens above $290.
- **Uber wheel:** paper-trade it first. Uber is a great **learning underlying**: liquid, active, affordable. Run 2–3 simulated cycles of a $62.50 put through November, including earnings, before committing $6,250 of real money.
