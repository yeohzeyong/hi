## 4. Rules (same as before)
- Margin of safety first: no buys above the buy line.
- Buy in tranches. Don't try to catch the exact bottom.
- **Thesis breaks:**
  - GOOG: Search share or Cloud margins slipping.
  - SPGI: a lasting fall in debt issuance.
  - AXP: write-offs above ~3%.
  - NVDA: a hyperscaler capex cut or gross margin < 65%.

- Review after the next earnings season (late Jan to early Feb 2027).

## Update: only 3 stocks (GOOG, NVDA + one). Is TSMC the third?

**TSMC (TSM) at ~$360** (Nov 17 close, after −3.7% on new US export rules):
- **Valuation:** ~16x forward EPS (GuruFocus, Nov 19). 2027 EPS consensus is ~$19.5–23, with ~27–29% growth → ~16–18x 2027 EPS.
- **Business:** Q3 gross margin 66.4%. October revenue was +59.7% year on year.
- **Moat:** wide. It makes almost every leading-edge AI chip.
- **My zones:** fair ~$420–440 (~20x 2027) · **buy ≤ ~$370** · strong buy ≤ ~$320.

**Verdict:** a good value on its own. But **as the third pick it adds no diversification.** Nvidia is TSMC's largest customer, and Google's TPUs are also made at TSMC. On Nov 5 all three fell together. GOOG + NVDA + TSM (+ your MSFT) is one bet on AI capex. TSMC also carries Taiwan geopolitical risk: Berkshire bought ~$4B in Q3 2022 and sold almost all of it within a quarter, with Buffett citing its location.

**Third pick: S&P Global (SPGI).** It has a wide moat (ratings duopoly), ~10–13% discount, and its earnings are driven by debt issuance, not AI capex. Backup choice: BRK.B (maximum safety).

**If you want TSMC anyway:** use it **instead of NVDA**, not as well as. It wins whether Nvidia, Google TPUs or anyone else wins, and it has no CEO crisis.
