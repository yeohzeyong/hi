## 1. Framework used (from the books you supplied)

### Pabrai's Dhandho checklist
1. Buy **existing** businesses with a track record.
2. Buy **simple** businesses in slowly changing industries.
3. Buy **distressed businesses in distressed industries**: the best odds come when fear is highest.
4. Buy businesses with a **durable moat**.
5. **Few bets, big bets, infrequent bets.**
6. Look for **arbitrage** spreads.
7. Always demand a **margin of safety**.
8. Prefer **low-risk, high-uncertainty** situations: the market treats uncertainty as if it were risk.
9. **Clone** rather than innovate. "Heads, I win; tails, I don't lose much!"

### Buffett/Hagstrom tenets
- **Business:** simple and understandable, consistent operating history, favorable long-term prospects (a moat).
- **Management:** rational capital allocation, candor, resists the "institutional imperative".
- **Financial:** high ROE/ROIC with little debt, owner earnings (FCF), high margins, and each $1 retained creating at least $1 of market value.
- **Value:** estimate intrinsic value and buy only at a meaningful discount.

### How I applied it
A stock made the buy list only if it passed all four checks:
1. **Moat:** a durable competitive advantage (wide or narrow).
2. **Conviction cloning:** owned or recently bought by at least one of the value gurus below, ideally several.
3. **Price dislocation:** trading well below its 52-week high or near its low, because of a *narrative* scare, not a broken business.
4. **Rough expected return:** earnings yield + sustainable growth of at least ~12–15%, assuming a constant multiple.

### Why SPGI ranks #1
- The **AI-disruption scare** hit S&P Global's *Market Intelligence* segment, but the cash engines are **Ratings** (a legal and reputational duopoly with Moody's) and **S&P Dow Jones Indices** (fee-on-AUM, network effects). Neither is easily replaced by an LLM.
- At ~19–20x earnings it trades at its lowest multiple since late 2022, versus a historical 25–30x for a business with 60%+ incremental margins and steady buybacks.
- Smart money moved in during Q2: Ackman opened a new position, TCI added, Kantesaria holds ~20%. That is Pabrai's "clone the best" setup.

### Why AXP ranks #2
- It is Buffett's 2nd-largest holding and has been held ~30 years. The stock is down 21%, near its 52-week low, after an expense ramp (marketing and tech) and stablecoin/rate-cap headlines.
- It still grows revenue ~10%, has a premium cardholder base with low credit losses, and the closed-loop model gives it data, pricing power and brand.

### Why BN ranks #3, with caution
- It trades about 45% below management's own sum-of-the-parts **plan value of ~$67/share**, and only ~3% above its 52-week low. Ackman (#2 position) and Akre own it.
- That is classic Dhandho, "low risk, high uncertainty": the private-credit fear is about sentiment, not impaired real assets.
- Risks: high consolidated leverage, opaque insurance float (Brookfield Wealth Solutions), the 2030 target slipping to 2031, and Akre's 29% trim. **Size it smaller than a pure toll-booth.**

### Why ELV, not UNH
- Elevance is at ~14x 2026 EPS, with guidance for at least $27 and a 12% EPS growth target for 2027. Medicare Advantage 2027 rates came in at +2.48%, much better than the 0.09% first proposed.

## 6. Suggested portfolio construction (Pabrai/Buffett style)

**Few bets, big bets, infrequent bets.** Hypothetical 8–10 position model:

| Bucket | Names | Target weight |
|---|---|---|
| Core toll-booths (wide moat, fair-to-cheap) | SPGI, AXP, MCO, V or MA | 45–50% |
| Discounted compounders (narrative fear) | BN, ELV, AON, CSU | 25–30% |
| Asymmetric "heads I win" bets (small) | PDD, KSPI, NFLX or UBER | 10–15% |
| Dry powder | Cash/T-bills, for when CAPE ~40 meets a real correction | 10–20% |

**Rules:**
1. Buy in 3 tranches. Add only if the price falls *and* the thesis holds.
2. Re-check each thesis on the next earnings report. For SPGI and MCO: ratings issuance and index AUM. For AXP: billings growth and write-offs. For BN: fundraising and plan-value updates. For ELV: medical-cost ratio.
3. Sell when the price exceeds intrinsic value by about 20–30%, or when the moat breaks (as FICO's has started to).
4. Mind single-country risk (PDD, KSPI) and position size accordingly.
